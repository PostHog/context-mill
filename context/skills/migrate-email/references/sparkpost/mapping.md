# SparkPost to PostHog mapping

This file decides, field by field, whether PostHog can send a SparkPost call site. A site moves only when every field it sets moves. Otherwise it stays on SparkPost, and the reason names the first field or feature that keeps it there. `api.md` has the PostHog side in full, and the rewrite file for the site's language, which Step 7 names, has PostHog's names in that language.

PostHog fields are written in their Node.js names here, such as `replyTo`. Use the name the rewrite file gives for the site's language, such as `reply_to` in Python.

**Tracking.** A moved send counts opens, and a send in the code form counts no clicks. No field changes that, as Step 7 says. A template made in PostHog counts clicks unless it is marked sensitive, as "Templates made in PostHog" says. Work out, for opens and for clicks, whether SparkPost tracked them. The first of these that sets one wins: the recipient's `options`, the transmission's `options`, the template's `options` for a template site, and then the account's settings, which `account.md` reads under "Tracking settings". There, `rest_tracking_default` decides both, and it is `true` unless the person turned it off, and the account's `click_tracking: false` turns clicks off. A site that sends from a subaccount with `open_tracking_disabled: true` tracked no opens, whatever it set. `initial_open` only adds a second open pixel at the top: it decides nothing on its own.

- Opens tracked: the site moves, and the tracking fields are dropped.
- Opens untracked, after the order above and the subaccount setting, such as `open_tracking: false` that no recipient's `options` turns back on, or a template, account, or subaccount default that leaves them off: the site's emails had no open pixel. It moves only with the person's yes, as Step 3 under "Opens the provider didn't track" says, and otherwise stays with `open tracking off`. Clicks don't change this.
- When its recipients' `options` disagree on open tracking, or it sets `initial_open: true` with open tracking off, nobody can tell whether its emails had an open pixel: SparkPost's docs don't say. The site stays, and the reason is `open tracking not checked`.
- An address with an `open_tracking` entry on the suppression list, a `Tracking Consent Revoked` entry, got no open tracking, and PostHog has no such setting per address. Ask the person once whether the list holds such entries, or read them as `account.md` says under "Suppression list". When the person can't say and the list can't be read, a site that moves with PostHog's open pixel stays, and the reason is `open tracking consent not checked`. When the list holds such entries, a site that moves with PostHog's open pixel stays, whether SparkPost tracked its opens or the person agreed to the pixel, and the reason is `open tracking consent revoked for some recipients`. So does such a site when the app itself can write an `open_tracking` entry, as "Calls that change the suppression list" says, with the reason `the app can revoke open tracking consent`: PostHog would keep tracking opens for an address the app revokes later. A click on a `data-msys-revoke-tracking` link writes such an entry too, for the account or subaccount that sent the email, unless the link also sets `data-msys-clicktrack="0"`. In the same ask, ask whether any email that account still sends through SparkPost carries such a link, such as a site that stays, a template, or mail sent from SparkPost's app. When one does, or the person can't say, a site of that account that moves with PostHog's open pixel stays, and the reason is `a SparkPost link can revoke open tracking consent`, or `open tracking consent not checked`. A link in mail SparkPost already delivered keeps working while SparkPost serves it, so also ask whether earlier mail of that account carried one. When it did, such a site stays too, and the reason is `a SparkPost link can revoke open tracking consent`. When the person can't say, it stays, and the reason is `open tracking consent not checked`.
- When the account or subaccount a site sends from minimizes its open events, its `enable_minimized_event_passthrough` setting as `account.md` reads it, a site that tracked opens stays, as a send that sets the field does: the setting wins over the send's own value, and PostHog's send doesn't take it yet. The reason is `open events minimized on the <account|subaccount>`. Check this for every site that tracked opens, also one that sets its own tracking. When the person can't say and the API can't be read, the site stays, and the reason is `open event minimization not checked`.
- Clicks: for every site that moves, whatever its opens were, when SparkPost tracked its clicks, or nobody knows whether it did, as `account.md` says under "Clicks", and the site moves to anything but a template made in PostHog that is not marked sensitive, note in the plan row that PostHog counts no clicks for it.

When a site leaves open tracking to the account, or sends from a subaccount, ask the person once for each account the moved sites send from, or read the settings as `account.md` says. When the answer is not given, nobody can tell whether the site's emails had an open pixel: the site stays, and the reason is `open tracking not checked`, as Step 3 under "Opens the provider didn't track" says.

These rules read each account's settings as they are today. The app's code can also change them, through `PUT /api/v1/account` or `PUT /api/v1/subaccounts/{id}`, or each SDK's call for them, and that call stays on SparkPost. Search the code for such calls. When one can change a setting this mapping read for an account a moved site sends from, such as its open tracking, its subaccount's `open_tracking_disabled`, its event minimization, or its `transactional_default` or `transactional_unsub`, the site stays, and the reason is `the app can change SparkPost's <setting>`: after the move, a later change would no longer reach its emails. Read what the call sets: a call that changes none of those settings keeps nothing.

### Cc and bcc

SparkPost has no `cc` or `bcc` field. A cc or bcc is one more entry in `recipients` whose `address.header_to` names the main recipient. A cc is also listed in a `CC` header in `content.headers`, and a bcc is not. The Node.js, PHP, and Python SDKs build this from their own `cc` and `bcc` fields, and the .NET library from `RecipientType.CC` and `RecipientType.BCC`. A site with a cc or bcc recipient stays, as the Fields table says.

## Calls

| SparkPost call | A site stays on SparkPost when |
|---|---|
| `client.transmissions.send`, `client.transmissions.send({ transmissionBody }, callback)` in Node.js 1.x, `sp.transmissions.send`, `send_message`, `send_payload`, `$sparky->transmissions->post`, `client.Send`, `client.SendContext`, `client.Transmissions.Send`, `SparkPost.Transmission.send`, `SparkPost.send`, `POST /api/v1/transmissions` | A field above keeps it |
| A transmission with several `recipients` without `header_to`, or a stored `list_id` | Always: it sends several emails in one call |
| SMTP through `smtp.sparkpostmail.com` or `smtp.eu.sparkpostmail.com` | Always. PostHog has no SMTP relay |
| A send that the code runs after it removes the recipient from SparkPost's suppression list: `DELETE /api/v1/suppression-list/{recipient}`, or the SDK's call for it, such as `client.suppressionList.delete` in Node.js | Always. The code wants the email to reach an address SparkPost suppressed, and PostHog refuses a suppressed address with `recipient_suppressed`. The removal call stays too |
| A framework mailer or transport (Django, Rails, Laravel, Swoosh, Bamboo, `nodemailer-sparkpost-transport`) | Always. PostHog has no mailer driver |
| A send in a language without a PostHog SDK that sends email, such as Java or Kotlin | Always. Step 7 lists the languages PostHog's SDKs cover |
| A send the code can cancel, such as a `fetch` or axios request with a `signal` a caller passes in or an `AbortController` it aborts, or Go's `client.SendContext` with a context a caller can cancel | Unless the PostHog send in that language takes the same cancellation, as Go's `Send(ctx, ...)` does: pass it there. Otherwise always, as in Node.js, whose send takes no signal: an email the old code cancelled would go out. A fixed deadline, such as `AbortSignal.timeout(...)` or `context.WithTimeout`, moves as "HTTP" says under "Errors" |

### Subaccounts

A site sends from the primary account, or from a subaccount when its API key belongs to that subaccount or the request sets the `X-MSYS-SUBACCOUNT` header. Each subaccount has its own suppression list, and can turn off open tracking for its mail. Ask the person once whether the SparkPost account has subaccounts. When it does, ask which account each key the app reads, and each `X-MSYS-SUBACCOUNT` value it sets, sends from, even when there is only one, and record each site's account under the sites table. Read that account's settings and suppression list, never the primary account's in its place.

SparkPost keeps an idempotency key per account, and PostHog per project. When the sites that move send from more than one account, two of them can send the same key for different emails, and PostHog would refuse the second. Then give each moved site's key its account as a prefix, such as `sub-<subaccount id>-` or `primary-`, and note in the plan row that the key changed. A site whose prefixed key can be longer than 255 characters stays, and the reason is `idempotency key can't carry its account`.

PostHog keeps one suppression list per project. When the sites that move send from more than one account, Step 5 imports the list of each of them into that one list. PostHog then refuses a moved send to an address that bounced in any of those accounts, even when the site's own account never suppressed it. Tell the person so, and ask once whether the moved sites may share one suppression list. When they say no, ask which one account's sites move. The sites of every other account stay, and the reason is `SparkPost account <name> keeps its own suppression list`.

### Suppression list

SparkPost keeps a suppression list, so Step 3 asks which moving sites rely on it to stop mailing an address someone added to it, as it says after the sender check, and the reason is `relies on SparkPost's suppression list`. In SparkPost, those are the `Manually Added` and `Compliance` entries, which someone added in the dashboard, and the addresses the app's calls add, as "Calls that change the suppression list" says. Ask about the list of each account the sites send from, as "Subaccounts" says. `List Unsubscribe` and `Unsubscribe Link` entries are unsubscribes from non-transactional mail, which `options.transactional` already asks about for each site they reach.

## Code around the send

SparkPost's SDKs report an error in two ways. The Node.js, Python, Ruby, PHP, and .NET SDKs throw or reject. PHP's may instead answer an error with a response, depending on the app's HTTP adapter, as `sdk-reference.md` says. The Go SDK returns an error value, and the Elixir SDK returns an error struct in place of the response. The rewrite file says under "The answer" how PostHog's SDK reports an error in its language. Keep the site failing the same way it did, as the Rewrite section says.

| Code | A site stays on SparkPost when |
|---|---|
| A `catch`, `except`, or `rescue` that reads `err.statusCode`, `err.errors`, `e.status`, `e.errors`, `getCode()`, `getBody()`, or `Response.StatusCode` | It branches on a SparkPost error `code`, such as `1902`, or on its text. A branch on the HTTP status moves: a branch on `400` or `422`, SparkPost's answers to an invalid send, reads PostHog's error name `validation_error`, a branch on `420`, its sending limit, reads PostHog's code `quota_limited`, a branch on `429`, such as a retry or a back-off, reads PostHog's status code `429` and a code other than `quota_limited`, and a branch on another status reads PostHog's status code, as the Rewrite section says |
| A callback `(err, data)`, or `(err, res)` in Node.js 1.x, that checks `err` | Never. Check PostHog's error instead |
| A check of `res.HTTP.StatusCode` in Go, or a match on `%SparkPost.Endpoint.Error{}` in Elixir | Never. Read PostHog's error instead, as the Rewrite section says |
| A check of `total_rejected_recipients` or `total_accepted_recipients` | Never. With one recipient, check PostHog's error instead |
| Code that reads a header of SparkPost's answer, from the response or the error the SDK gives, such as `res.HTTP.Header` in Go, or from the answer of the site's own HTTP call | When it reads anything but the retry delay of a refused send, a `Retry-After` header: PostHog's answer carries no headers, and its error carries only the retry delay, as Step 7 says. The reason is `reads <header> from SparkPost's answer`. The retry delay moves as "The retry delay" says under "Rewrite". A header the code takes from the answer and never uses goes away |
| `results.id`, the transmission id, stored for a webhook, a status read, or a cancel by campaign | Always, when the webhook handler, the Events API, or the cancel uses it |
| `results.id`, or `res.body.results.id` in Node.js 1.x, logged or stored for the app's own use | SparkPost's id is a decimal number, and PostHog's message id is a UUID, a string of 36 characters, as Step 7 says. Where the code keeps the id as a string, or only logs it, use PostHog's message id. When it parses the id as a number, or the id reaches a numeric column, the site stays, and the reason is `stores the transmission id as a number`. A UUID column takes it, and so does a text column of at least 36 characters. When the id reaches a shorter text column, one whose length the code doesn't show, or another service that expects SparkPost's id, ask the person once, for all such sites, whether PostHog's message id fits it. A site they can't confirm stays, and the reason is `passes the transmission id where PostHog's id may not fit` |

## Content

SparkPost runs inline content through its template language too, with values from `substitution_data`. Read the site's `subject`, `from`, `reply_to`, `text`, and `html` for `{{`, and its `html` and `text` for `data-msys-` and `[[data-msys-`. With `options.perform_substitutions: false`, read only `subject`, `from`, and `reply_to` for `{{`: `text` and `html` go as they are, and a `{{` in them is text.

- No template language: the content moves as it is.
- Inline content with template language moves into the code too: the code renders the site's `subject`, `from`, `reply_to`, `text`, and `html` with the "Templates" table before the send, from the values the site puts into `substitution_data`, and the send carries no `substitution_data`. Where the transmission and its one recipient both set a key, the recipient's `substitution_data` wins over the transmission's. A part the table doesn't place keeps the site, and the reason names it.

**The plain-text part.** PostHog makes a text part from the HTML whenever a send has no text, as `template-to-code.md` says. SparkPost's docs say only that `html` or `text` is required, and not whether SparkPost made a text part for an email without one. So a site whose email has `html` and no `text`, or a `text` the code can leave empty, from inline content or a template version, moves only when SparkPost made one too. Ask the person once, for all such sites, to open the source of one email SparkPost sent them without text, such as from an html-only site or from a branch where `text` was empty, and check whether it holds a `text/plain` part. An email that carried the site's own text shows nothing about this, so it doesn't count. When the email holds one, the sites move. The send keeps the site's `text` where it sets one, passes it on the branches where it isn't empty, and leaves it out where it is missing or empty, so the send has `html` only there. When it doesn't, they stay, and the reason is `turns the plain-text part off`. When they can't check, they stay, and the reason is `plain-text part not checked`.

## Templates

A stored template site moves by bringing the template into the code, as `template-to-code.md` says. The check needs the content of the version the site sends, the published one unless it sets `use_draft_template: true`, because the code has only its id. `account.md` says how to get it, and how to get a snippet the template renders. The code renders the version's `subject`, `from`, `reply_to`, `html`, and `text` with the site's `substitution_data`, the recipient's values winning over the transmission's, and the send sets the rendered `subject`, `html`, `text`, `from`, and `replyTo`. Inline content uses the same language, and moves by the same table, as "Content" says.

SparkPost's template language, in the code:

| SparkPost | In the code |
|---|---|
| `{{name}}`, `{{ name }}` | The value, escaped in `html`, and as it is in `text`, the subject, `from`, and `reply_to`. A missing or `null` value renders as nothing. A number renders as the JSON number the site sent, such as `5` or `39.99`, never `5.0` |
| `{{user.first_name}}`, `{{user['first_name']}}`, `{{user[part]}}` | The nested value, by a fixed key or by the key that another value holds, escaped the same way. A missing step of the path renders as nothing |
| `{{items[1]}}`, `{{items[1].name}}` | The item at that position, counting from 1, so `items[0]` in a language that counts from 0. A position past the end renders as nothing |
| `{{{name}}}` | The value as it is, unescaped, under the rule of `template-to-code.md`. In `text` and the subject, it is the plain value |
| `{{ name or 'there' }}` | The value, or `there` when the value is missing or `null`. An empty string stays empty. When the value can be `false`, the template stays: the docs don't say what `or` does with it |
| `{{if a}}…{{elseif b}}…{{else}}…{{end}}`, with an optional `then` after a condition | A branch that tests each condition in order, with SparkPost's truth test below |
| `==`, `!=` in a condition | A comparison of two strings, or of two numbers. When the two sides can differ in type, such as `3` and `"3"`, the template stays: the docs don't say how they compare |
| `<`, `>`, `<=`, `>=` in a condition | A comparison of two numbers. On any other value the template stays |
| `and`, `or`, `not` in a condition | The same logic, each side tested with the truth test. A condition that mixes `and` with `or`, or puts `not` before a comparison, without parentheses, keeps the template: the docs don't say how they bind |
| `{{#items}}` | The number of entries in the list. A value that is not always a list keeps the template |
| `+`, `-`, `*` | The same arithmetic, on whole numbers. `/`, or a value that is not always a whole number, keeps the template: the docs don't say how the result is written |
| `{{each items}}…{{end}}` | A loop over the list, in order. A missing, `null`, or empty list renders nothing. A value that can be anything else keeps the template |
| Inside `{{each}}`: `{{loop_var}}`, `{{loop_var.name}}` | The current item, or its field |
| Inside nested `{{each}}`: `{{loop_vars.cart.name}}` | The current item of the loop over `cart`, named by the last part of the loop's path, such as `a_nested_array` for `{{each loop_vars.cart.a_nested_array}}` |
| Inside `{{each}}`: a bare `{{name}}` | Keeps the template: SparkPost's docs read the item only through `loop_var`, and don't say whether a bare name reads the value outside the loop or a field of the item |
| `{{loop_index}}` | Keeps the template: the docs don't say whether it counts from 0 or from 1 |
| `empty(items)`, as in `{{if not empty(items)}}` | Whether the list has no entries. On a value that can be anything but a list, the template stays |
| `render_dynamic_content(dynamic_html.offer)`, also with `dynamic_plain` or `dynamic_html[loop_var]` | The chunk the site passes under `dynamic_html` in the transmission's `substitution_data`, or under `dynamic_plain` in `text`, rendered with this table as its own small template and inserted as it is. A chunk the code doesn't build from literals, such as one read from a database, or one under a recipient's `substitution_data`, keeps the template: its template language can't be checked |
| `render_snippet("footer")` | The snippet, brought into the code as a piece the emails share: its `html` in `html` and its `text` in `text`, rendered with the same values and inserted as it is. A snippet id from a value moves when the code sets that value from literals only: the code picks among the snippets it knows, and raises on any other id, as SparkPost failed the send. A snippet without the part the content needs, or whose content the person can't give, keeps the template. So does a snippet that the app's own code creates, changes, or deletes through `/api/labs/snippets`, as `template-to-code.md` says for a template the app changes, and the reason is `the app changes this snippet at SparkPost` |
| `opening_double_curly()` and the other brace macros | The braces as text: `{`, `}`, `{{`, `}}`, `{{{`, or `}}}` |
| `{{address.name}}` | The one recipient's name |
| `{{address.email}}`, `{{email}}`, `{{email_id}}` | The one recipient's address: SparkPost fills all three with it |
| A statement (`if`, `elseif`, `else`, `end`, `each`) on its own line | No line in the output, and no whitespace after the statement. Build `text` the same way, so its lines match |
| An expression in a link URL, such as `href="https://acme.dev/r?t={{token}}"` | The value URL-encoded, each byte other than letters, digits, `-`, `.`, `_`, and `~` written as `%XX`, then escaped as HTML in `html`. `{{{ }}}` in a URL inserts it as it is. The docs don't say which characters SparkPost leaves as they are: when the value can hold one outside that set, note it in the plan row |
| A link whose `https://` comes from a value, such as `href="{{url}}"` | The value escaped as HTML, with no URL encoding. SparkPost doesn't treat it as a link, and its docs don't say how it escapes it: note it in the plan row |
| `data-msys-linkname` | Dropped. PostHog counts no clicks, so it has no use for a link name |
| `data-msys-clicktrack="0"`, `[[data-msys-clicktrack="0"]]` in `text` | Dropped. PostHog tracks no link of a code send, as "Tracking" says |
| The version's `subject`, `from`, and `reply_to` | The send's `subject`, `from`, and `replyTo`, rendered with no escaping. A `from` of `{ name, email }` becomes `'Name <email>'`, as the Fields table says |
| The version's `text` | The send's `text`, with no escaping. A version without `text` sends `html` only, when "The plain-text part" under "Content" lets it move |

- SparkPost's truth test: a block renders for a value that is not `false`, `null`, or missing. The docs don't say how `0`, `""`, or an empty list test. When a value that an `if` tests on its own can be one of them at run time, the template stays. Write the test out, as `template-to-code.md` says, such as `value !== false && value != null` in Node.js.
- When the transmission and the recipient set one key to two objects, and the template reads a field the recipient's object doesn't have, the template stays: the docs don't say whether the two objects merge.
- Anything else keeps the template on SparkPost: the reserved values `env_from` and `return_path`, `{{else}}` inside `{{each}}`, any other macro, and the link attributes `data-msys-unsubscribe` (SparkPost records the unsubscribe from the click), `data-msys-revoke-tracking`, and `data-msys-sublink` (the tracked path serves app links), in `html` or as `[[...]]` in `text`. So does a version with `headers`, an `amp_html` part, or `email_rfc822` content, a `reply_to` that carries a name, and a `transactional` setting of `false` that applies. The docs name no `opt_out` value or other tag that SparkPost fills with an unsubscribe link: a `{{opt_out}}` is a value the site passes, and renders like any other.
- SparkPost's docs say it stores no images, so a template's images sit on hosts the person chose. Keep every URL. An image on a SparkPost host, whose name ends in `sparkpost.com` or `sparkpostmail.com`, serves only while the account does: list it, as `template-to-code.md` says.
- A template's `options` decide tracking and `transactional` under the transmission's, as "Tracking" and the Fields table say. A template site follows "Tracking" like any other site.
- `override.from` and `override.reply_to` replace the template's `from` and `reply_to`: the send takes the override. `metadata`, `campaign_id`, `recipients[].tags`, and the other fields next to `template_id` keep the site or move as the Fields table says.
- `use_draft_template: true` sends the draft, which anyone in the SparkPost account can still change. Ask the person once whether the draft as it is now is the email to keep in the code. When they say yes, bring the draft in. Otherwise the site stays, and the reason is `sends a SparkPost draft that can still change`.

### Templates made in PostHog

Only for a template the person asks to make in PostHog, as Step 3 says. The person makes it from the SparkPost template and gives you its template ID, and makes it transactional once a verified sender covers its address, as Step 3 says. Until they give the ID, the site stays on SparkPost. A site with `use_draft_template: true` stays: it sends a draft that can still change. SparkPost's template language becomes Liquid in the PostHog template. Values move under `variables`, and each name keeps its case:

| SparkPost | PostHog |
|---|---|
| `{{name}}` | `{{ variables.name }}` |
| `{{user.first_name}}`, `{{user['first_name']}}` | `{{ variables.user.first_name }}` |
| `{{ name or 'there' }}` | `{{ variables.name \| default: 'there' }}` |
| `{{if paid}}…{{elseif due}}…{{else}}…{{end}}`, when `paid` and `due` are booleans | `{% if variables.paid %}…{% elsif variables.due %}…{% else %}…{% endif %}` |
| `{{if not paid}}…{{end}}`, when `paid` is a boolean | `{% unless variables.paid %}…{% endunless %}` |
| `{{if plan == "pro"}}`, with `!=`, `<`, `>`, `<=`, or `>=` | `{% if variables.plan == "pro" %}`, with the same operator |
| `{{if paid and active}}`, `{{if paid or trial}}`, with one `and` or one `or`, when each side is a boolean or a comparison | `{% if variables.paid and variables.active %}`, `{% if variables.paid or variables.trial %}` |
| `{{each items}}{{loop_var.name}}{{end}}`, when `items` is a list | `{% for item in variables.items %}{{ item.name }}{% endfor %}` |
| `{{each tags}}{{loop_var}}{{end}}`, when `tags` is a list | `{% for tag in variables.tags %}{{ tag }}{% endfor %}` |
| `{{address.name}}`, `{{address.email}}`, `{{email}}`, `{{email_id}}` | `{{ variables.recipient_name }}`, `{{ variables.recipient_email }}`: the site adds the recipient's name or address to `templateData` under that name. When `substitution_data` already has a key by that name, pick one it doesn't have, such as `sparkpost_recipient_email`, and use it in both places |
| The published version's `subject` | The PostHog template's subject, with the same conversions |

Write the `\|` above as a plain `|`. The table escapes it.

- SparkPost escapes HTML in values in `html`, and so does PostHog's Liquid, so plain values render the same there.
- SparkPost escapes values in `html` and `amp_html` only, not in `text` or the subject. When a value in the text part or the subject can hold `&`, `<`, `>`, or quotes, such as a name, note in the report that the person checks the text part and the subject in a test send.
- SparkPost's `if` skips its block for `false` and `null` only, and its docs don't say how `0`, an empty string, or an empty list behave. Liquid treats them as true. Read what the site sends for each value an `if` tests on its own. A value that is not always a boolean, `null`, or missing keeps the template on SparkPost.
- SparkPost's `each` walks a list. An `each` over a value that is not always a list keeps the template on SparkPost.
- `{{{name}}}` inserts HTML as it is. PostHog's Liquid escapes it, so a template that uses triple braces stays on SparkPost.
- SparkPost reads the current item of an `each` through `loop_var`, and its docs don't say what a bare `{{name}}` inside `each` reads. A bare name inside `each` keeps the template on SparkPost. `{{loop_var}}` and `{{loop_var.name}}` become `{{ item }}` and `{{ item.name }}`, never `{{ variables.name }}`.
- An expression inside a link URL converts only when the value holds URL-safe characters only, such as an id or a token the app makes. SparkPost URL-encodes it, and the conversion above inserts it as it is.
- Anything else keeps the template on SparkPost: `loop_index`, `loop_vars` in nested loops, `#` for a length, an array index, arithmetic, `not` inside a longer condition, more than one `and` or `or`, a macro such as `render_dynamic_content`, `render_snippet`, `empty`, or a brace macro, the reserved values `env_from` and `return_path`, and any `data-msys-*` link attribute or `[[data-msys-...]]` in `text` other than `data-msys-clicktrack="0"` and `data-msys-linkname`, which are dropped as in "Templates".
- A template whose `from` or `reply_to` holds a template expression, or whose version has `headers` or an `amp_html` part, stays.
- A template keeps its own `options`: `open_tracking`, `click_tracking`, and `transactional`. A transmission option the site sets wins over the template's, and the account's setting applies when neither sets one. A template site follows "Tracking" like any other site. When the `transactional` that applies is `false`, the site stays, and when neither sets it, the account's `transactional_default` applies, as the Fields table says for `options.transactional`.
- An email that carries a login code, a reset link, or another secret needs its PostHog template marked sensitive: PostHog then keeps no copy of each sent email's body or filled-in subject, and sends its links as they are, without its click redirect. Templates are not sensitive by default.
- A template made in PostHog that is not marked sensitive sends each link through PostHog's click redirect and counts its clicks. SparkPost didn't track the site's clicks when "Tracking" finds them off, or a link of the template sets `data-msys-clicktrack="0"`. Nobody knows whether it did when the account's settings decide and `account.md` couldn't read them. Step 3 then asks the person to mark the template sensitive, as it says under "A template in PostHog".
- Liquid's `default` also replaces an empty string, and SparkPost's `or` does not. When the code can pass an empty string for such a value, the email shows the fallback. Note it in the report.
- The PostHog template carries its own sender, reply-to address, and subject. A site that sets `override.from` or `override.reply_to` next to `template_id` moves only when the value is the same in every send, and the person puts it into the PostHog template. Otherwise it stays.
- A site that sets `metadata`, `campaign_id`, or `recipients[].tags` next to `template_id` stays: a template made in PostHog sends with its own settings, and the template form takes none of them. `options.open_tracking`, `options.initial_open`, `options.click_tracking`, and `recipients[].options` next to `template_id` decide the tracking that applies, as the bullet above says, and are dropped.

## Webhooks

The event webhook stops getting events for a site that moves. PostHog records the events of a moved send on the person it names with `distinctId`, when engagement capture is on:

| SparkPost event `type` | PostHog event |
|---|---|
| `injection` | `$workflows_email_sent` |
| `delivery` | `$workflows_email_delivered` |
| `bounce`, `out_of_band` | `$workflows_email_bounced` |
| `spam_complaint` | `$workflows_email_blocked` |
| `open`, `initial_open` | `$workflows_email_opened` |
| `click` | `$workflows_email_link_clicked`, only for a template made in PostHog that is not marked sensitive. A send in the code form counts no clicks |
| `policy_rejection`, `generation_failure`, `generation_rejection` | none. PostHog refuses the send instead, such as with `recipient_suppressed` |
| `delay`, `list_unsubscribe`, `link_unsubscribe`, `amp_open`, `amp_initial_open`, `amp_click` | none |

PostHog's events carry no SparkPost `transmission_id`, `message_id`, `rcpt_meta`, `rcpt_tags`, `campaign_id`, or `template_id`. A site whose events a handler matches by one of them stays on SparkPost. For every other handler, such as one that reads only `type` and `rcpt_to`, the report tells the person which events it handles, which PostHog events take their place, and that a webhook destination in PostHog (Data pipelines, then Destinations) can post them to an endpoint. The handler itself keeps working for the sites that stay. Never change it.

A site whose emails a handler acts on for an event that has no PostHog event in the table, such as `delay`, `list_unsubscribe`, or `policy_rejection`, stays, and the reason is `its <event> events feed a webhook handler`. A site whose emails a `click` handler acts on moves only to a template made in PostHog that is not marked sensitive, since a send in the code form counts no clicks. Otherwise it stays, and the reason is `its clicks feed a webhook handler`.

## Rewrite

How a site that moves is written with PostHog's SDK. Step 7 says when to use each part, and the rewrite file for the site's language has the client and the send.

**The PostHog client.** Set `migratedFrom: 'sparkpost'` on the client that Step 7 configures, when its client options take it.

**The call.** The SparkPost transmission becomes one PostHog send. Build its fields from the transmission the site built:

- Node.js, PHP, and HTTP: `content.from`, `content.subject`, `content.html`, `content.text`, `content.reply_to`, and the one recipient. In Node.js 1.x they sit under `transmissionBody`, and the rewrite drops that wrapper. The 1.x SDK also renamed keys before the send, as `sdk-reference.md` says: classify the site and render its values by the names SparkPost got, so `substitutionData: { firstName }` fills `{{first_name}}`, and becomes `templateData: { first_name }` for a template made in PostHog.
- Python `send(recipients=, from_email=, subject=, html=, text=, reply_to=)`: the same fields.
- Ruby `send_message`: the same fields, as SparkPost got them. A positional argument names no field, and a keyword such as `content:` or `recipients:` can set a field a positional argument also sets. Read the gem installed in the app's bundle for which field each argument becomes, and which value wins where two set the same field, map the fields SparkPost got, and name the gem file you read in the plan row. When the installed gem doesn't settle it, the site stays, and the reason is `installed SparkPost gem doesn't show its send_message fields`.
- Go `sp.Transmission{Recipients, Content: sp.Content{From, Subject, HTML, Text, ReplyTo}}`: the same fields.
- .NET `transmission.Content` and `transmission.Recipients`: the same fields.
- Elixir `%Transmission{recipients:, content: %Content.Inline{...}}` or `SparkPost.send(to:, from:, subject:, text:, html:)`: the same fields.
- A template site names its template by `content.template_id`, Python's `template=`, the `template_id` key of Go's content map, .NET's `Content.TemplateId`, or Elixir's `%Content.TemplateRef{}`. Its `subject`, `from`, `reply_to`, `html`, and `text` come from the email brought into the code.
- `override.from` and `override.reply_to` replace the content's `from` and `reply_to`, for inline content as for a template: where the site sets one, the send takes it in place of the content's value. When `override.from` gives an address without a name and the content's or template's `from` has a name, SparkPost's docs don't say which name the email shows. Ask the person to check one such email they received: the send takes the address of `override.from` with the name that email shows, or none when it shows none. When they can't check, the site stays, and the reason is `from name not checked`.

**The fields.**

- `{ email: 'hello@mail.example.com', name: 'Example' }` in `from` becomes `'Example <hello@mail.example.com>'`. Put the name in double quotes when it holds a comma, a quote, or another special character, such as `'"Acme, Inc." <hello@mail.example.com>'`. A string `from` stays the same string.
- The one recipient's address becomes `to`, as a bare address. When a helper takes a list of recipients and every caller passes one address, change the parameter to one address and update the callers. Never send one email per address in a loop.
- The tracking fields are dropped, and the send gets no `captureEngagement` for them, as "Tracking" says.
- The `Idempotency-Key` header becomes `idempotencyKey`, with the same value.
- A send with rendered content gets a `templateKey`, as Step 7 says.

**Inline content with template language.** Render it in the code before the send, as "Templates" says, from the same values the site put into `substitution_data`, and drop `substitution_data` from the send. A short content renders at the site. A long one, or one with statements, becomes an email file, as `template-to-code.md` says under "Where the email goes":

- Apply the fallback of an `or` first: `{{ name or 'there' }}` becomes the value with a fallback for a missing or `null` value only, such as `name ?? 'there'` in Node.js or `'there' if name is None else name` in Python.
- Then turn each value into a string, and a missing or `null` value that has no fallback into an empty string, as SparkPost rendered it. Every escape helper takes a string: Python's `html.escape(42)` raises.
- In `html`, escape the value with the escape `template-to-code.md` names for the language, or the app's own helper. In `text`, `subject`, `from`, and `reply_to`, insert the value as it is. When the value is the name in `from`, quote it as "The fields" says.
- Note each site that got an escape helper in the plan.

**Templates brought into the code.** The site renders the email from its `substitution_data`, the recipient's values winning, as "Templates" says, and sends `templateKey`, the rendered `subject`, `html`, and `text`, and the template's `from` and `reply_to` as `from` and `replyTo`, or the site's `override.from` and `override.reply_to` where it sets them. It sends no `templateId` and no `templateData`.

**Templates made in PostHog.** The site sends `templateId` with the template ID the person gave, and `templateData` with the site's `substitution_data` and the recipient's `substitution_data` merged, the recipient's values winning. It sends no `templateKey`, `subject`, `html`, `text`, `from`, or `replyTo`: the template holds them.

**Errors.** Keep the site failing the same way it did. SparkPost answers an invalid send with `400` or `422`. A branch on either becomes a branch on PostHog's error name `validation_error`, never on a status code, after the `recipient_suppressed` branch below: PostHog's statuses for invalid sends differ from SparkPost's. SparkPost answers a send over the account's daily, monthly, or sandbox sending limit with `420`. A branch on `420` becomes a branch on PostHog's code `quota_limited`. PostHog answers a used-up quota with status `429` too, and no retry clears it, while SparkPost's `429` was a rate limit only. So a branch on `429`, such as a retry or a back-off, reads PostHog's status code `429` and a code other than `quota_limited`, and the quota error goes where SparkPost's `420` went. SparkPost answers `409` both for an `Idempotency-Key` whose first send is still in flight and for a key reused with a different payload. A branch on `409` becomes a branch on PostHog's error names `concurrent_idempotent_requests` and `invalid_idempotent_request`, never on a status code. A branch on another status, such as `401`, reads PostHog's status code. This holds in every language below.


- **Node.js.** `posthog.emails.send` never throws on an API error: it answers `{ data, error }`. Where `client.transmissions.send` rejected into a `catch`, throw from the error so the same `catch` runs: `if (error) throw Object.assign(new Error(error.message), { statusCode: error.statusCode, name: error.name, code: error.code, retryAfterMs: error.retryAfterMs })`. `err.statusCode` keeps holding the HTTP status, `err.retryAfterMs` carries the retry delay "The retry delay" says the `catch` reads, and a network failure, which has none, has `err.code` `network_error`, and a check of `err.statusCode === 400` or `422` becomes a check of `err.name === 'validation_error'`. A read of `err.errors` becomes a read of `err.message`. A callback `(err, data)` becomes the same code after the send, with PostHog's `error` as `err`. A branch on one status keeps that status only, and every other error goes where it went before.
- **Python.** PostHog's SDK raises too. Replace `except SparkPostAPIException` with the PostHog exception the rewrite file names, `e.status` with its status code, and `e.errors` with its message. A read of the error's response headers follows the header row in "Code around the send". Where the `except` handled one kind of error only, check for the same kind and raise every other error again: `e.status == 429` checks the status code `429` and a code other than `quota_limited`, and `e.status in (400, 422)` checks the name `validation_error`.
- **Ruby.** PostHog's SDK raises too. Replace `rescue SparkPost::DeliveryException` with the PostHog exception the rewrite file names. Where the `rescue` handled one kind of error only, check for the same kind with PostHog's `name`, `code`, or status code, and raise every other error again.
- **PHP.** PostHog's SDK throws, and sends at once. Where the installed package and the app's adapter answered a refused send with a response instead of throwing, as `sdk-reference.md` says, the site's check of that response, such as of its status code, becomes a check of PostHog's error: catch PostHog's exception around the send alone and run the code the check ran, as the HTTP bullet says for a call that answered a refused send without raising. Where SparkPost threw, as follows. Replace `catch (SparkPostException $e)` with the PostHog exception the rewrite file names, `getCode()` with its status code, and `getBody()['errors']` with `getMessage()`. A `->wait()` on the SparkPost promise goes away. A site that starts several sends before it waits on them, such as promises collected in a loop and settled together afterwards, stays, and the reason is `sends SparkPost promises concurrently`: PostHog's send would run them one after another. A `->then($ok, $fail)` becomes a `try` around the send alone, with the `$fail` code in its `catch`, and the `$ok` code after the `try`, run only when the send went out, such as with a flag the `try` sets: an exception the `$ok` code throws never reaches `$fail`, as with SparkPost's promise, so a `$fail` that retries never sends twice. Where the `catch` handled one status only, check for the same kind and throw every other error again: `getCode()` of `400` or `422` becomes `getErrorName() === 'validation_error'`, and another status reads `getStatusCode()`.
- **Go.** SparkPost answered `(id, res, err)`, and PostHog answers `(result, err)`. `id` becomes `result.ID`. Keep each branch on `err`. A read of `res.HTTP.StatusCode` becomes PostHog's status code, read with `errors.As` on the `*posthog.EmailError`. A read of `res.HTTP.Header` follows the header row in "Code around the send". A branch on `400` or `422` reads `emailErr.Name == "validation_error"`, and a branch on another status reads `emailErr.StatusCode`. Every other error goes on to the branch that handled it before.
- **.NET.** PostHog's SDK throws too. Replace `catch (ResponseException e)` with the PostHog exception the rewrite file names, and `e.Response.StatusCode` with its status code. A `catch` with a `when` filter on one status keeps the filter for the same kind, so every other error goes past it as before: `400` or `422` becomes a check of PostHog's error name `validation_error`, and another status checks its status code.
- **Elixir.** SparkPost answered the response struct or an error struct, and PostHog answers `{:ok, result}` or `{:error, error}`. A match on `%SparkPost.Transmission.Response{id: id}` becomes `{:ok, %PostHog.Emails.SendResult{id: id}}`, and a match on `%SparkPost.Endpoint.Error{status_code: code}` becomes `{:error, %PostHog.Emails.Error{status_code: code}}`. A match on one status keeps the same kind in the pattern, and every other error goes to the clause that took it before: `status_code: 400` or `422` becomes `name: :validation_error`, and another status keeps `status_code:`.
- **HTTP.** A raw call to `/api/v1/transmissions` raised on a refused send only when its client did: `axios` unless its `validateStatus` lets the status through, `got` or `ky` unless `throwHttpErrors: false`, Guzzle unless `http_errors: false`, Faraday with `raise_error`, or a call to `raise_for_status()` or `EnsureSuccessStatusCode()`. Such a site raises PostHog's error the same way, and its status reads follow the rules above. `fetch`, `requests`, `httpx`, `Net::HTTP`, Go's `net/http`, and .NET's `HttpClient` answered a refused send without raising: a status check becomes a check of PostHog's error, and a site that never checked the status logs PostHog's error and goes on, and raises or returns it when its code is `network_error`, as Step 7 says. A request with its own deadline keeps it, and a request whose limits the SDK can't keep stays, as Step 7 says under "Set up the client". For a raw call, the deadline is `signal: AbortSignal.timeout(5000)`, the `timeout` of Guzzle, `requests`, or `httpx`, `Net::HTTP`'s timeouts, or Faraday's on its default `net_http` adapter, as Ruby's rewrite file says under "A deadline", got's `timeout.request` or got 11's `timeout: 5000`, a Go `http.Client`'s `Timeout` or a context with a deadline, or .NET's `HttpClient.Timeout` or a `CancellationTokenSource` with a delay. So does a Go SparkPost send through `client.SendContext` with such a context, or through a SparkPost client whose `Client` is an `http.Client` with a `Timeout` or a `Transport` with its own limits, which Go's rewrite file keeps. Besides the limits Step 7 and Ruby's rewrite file name, such as axios's and ky's `timeout`, these limit one part of the request apart from the rest, and the SDK can't keep them, as Step 7 says: got's other `timeout` keys, `Net::HTTP`'s or Faraday's timeouts that Ruby's rewrite file keeps on SparkPost, such as only some of them set, any of Faraday's timeout options on an adapter other than `net_http`, as Ruby's rewrite file says for its `timeout`, and Guzzle's `read_timeout`. Only a limit the code sets counts, as Step 7 says: `Net::HTTP`'s default of 60 seconds is not one either.
- In Python, Ruby, and .NET, SparkPost's error class covers an error answer from the API only: a network error raised the HTTP library's own error, past the `except`, `rescue`, or `catch`. In Node.js, PHP, Go, and Elixir, SparkPost's error covered network errors too, so nothing changes there. PostHog's error also covers a network error, with the code `network_error`. Where the old catch named only SparkPost's class, raise an error whose code is `network_error` again, so it goes where it went before.
- PostHog refuses a send to a suppressed address with `recipient_suppressed`, where SparkPost took it and rejected it later. When the site throws, retries, or reports a failure on any error, handle `recipient_suppressed` before that branch the way the site handled a send that went out: log it and go on. A suppressed send has no message id: when the code after the send uses the id for more than a log line, such as returning it to its callers or storing it, the site stays, and the reason is `uses the message id of a suppressed send`, unless its callers already handle a send without an id. Note each site that got this branch in the report.
- When the same `try`, `begin`, or error check also covers a call that stays on SparkPost, such as `client.suppressionList.upsert` or `client.templates.get`, keep SparkPost's handler for that call and add PostHog's next to it.

**The retry delay.** A site that reads the `Retry-After` header of a refused send, as "Code around the send" says, reads the PostHog error's retry delay instead, under the name and in the unit its rewrite file gives under "The answer". The header is a string, and HTTP lets it hold seconds or a date: convert to the type and unit PostHog's SDK gives, where the code parsed a date, as the seconds until it. SparkPost's docs don't say when SparkPost sends the header, and PostHog sets the delay only when its answer named a wait, so keep the path the old code gave a missing header. A send through a client with retries has already waited and retried a rate limit by itself by then. A site that keeps its own deadline sends with no retries, as Step 7 says under "Set up the client", so its own wait and retry stay as they are.

**The answer.** The message id of a moved send that goes out is PostHog's. Code that logged `results.id`, or `res.body.results.id` in Node.js 1.x, or stored it for the app's own use, takes PostHog's message id instead.

**The SparkPost client.** Keep the SparkPost client, its import, and its setup while any site or other call still uses them. When nothing uses them, remove the import and the client, and keep the package and its key: the person removes them after the cutover. The SparkPost SDKs serve SparkPost only. The framework mailers that can send through SparkPost, such as `django-anymail`, Swoosh, and Bamboo, also serve other providers and the app's other mail: they always stay, and Step 9 never lists them for removal.

## Calls that change the suppression list

Step 3 and Step 5 say what to do with each of these calls. They are not sends, and they stay on SparkPost.

| Adds an address | Removes an address |
|---|---|
| `PUT /api/v1/suppression-list`, `PUT /api/v1/suppression-list/{recipient}`, and each SDK's call for them, such as `client.suppressionList.upsert` in Node.js | `DELETE /api/v1/suppression-list/{recipient}`, and each SDK's call for it, such as `client.suppressionList.delete` in Node.js |

A write of type `open_tracking` blocks no send: "Tracking" says what it does to a site. The account each call writes to is the one "Subaccounts" names for its key or `X-MSYS-SUBACCOUNT` value.
